**Affiliate Marketing Guidelines**

**Consumer Financial Services: Personal Loans, Credit Cards & Mortgage
Prequalification**

*Working compliance standard for affiliate content, reviews,
comparisons, rankings, and recommendations*

Purpose. This document provides a practical baseline for evaluating
affiliate marketing content promoting consumer financial products. It is
designed for use by marketers, affiliate partners, content reviewers,
and automated/LLM-based evaluation systems. It is not legal advice and
does not replace review by qualified legal, compliance, fair-lending,
privacy, or product counsel. Product-specific disclosures, state
requirements, lender/issuer policies, and contractual affiliate-program
rules may impose additional requirements.

# 1. Core principles

-   Truthful and non-misleading: Claims must accurately describe the
    product and must not omit material information in a way that creates
    a misleading overall impression.

-   Substantiated: Objective claims should be supported by reliable
    evidence that is appropriate to the claim. Do not invent rates,
    fees, approval odds, savings, benefits, or customer outcomes.

-   Current and product-specific: Use current, approved product terms.
    Do not assume that a rate, fee, reward, underwriting criterion, or
    eligibility rule applies universally.

-   Clear affiliate disclosure: Consumers should understand when the
    publisher or affiliate may receive compensation. The disclosure
    should be clear, conspicuous, and placed close enough to the
    recommendation or link to be noticed.

-   No implied guarantees: Avoid language implying guaranteed approval,
    guaranteed savings, guaranteed credit improvement, guaranteed
    home-purchase outcomes, or a guaranteed rate unless the statement is
    literally and legally supportable.

-   Consumer-first presentation: Compensation should not be the sole
    basis for presenting a product as better, cheaper, safer, or more
    suitable.

-   Fair and consistent treatment: Content and recommendation logic
    should not discriminate or use prohibited or inappropriate
    characteristics to steer consumers toward or away from credit
    products.

-   Transparent limitations: State important qualifications,
    restrictions, eligibility conditions, geographic limitations, and
    material exclusions when relevant to the claim.

# 2. Affiliate disclosure standards

Affiliate relationships are material connections when they could affect
how consumers evaluate a recommendation. The disclosure should be
understandable to an ordinary consumer and placed near the
recommendation or affiliate link.

## Recommended disclosure examples

-   "We may receive compensation when you click links and apply for or
    obtain a product. This compensation does not determine our editorial
    recommendations."

-   "Some links on this page are affiliate links, which means we may
    earn compensation if you apply through them."

-   For a comparison page: "We may be compensated by the companies
    featured on this page. Compensation may affect where products
    appear, but it does not change the factual terms presented."

Avoid relying on vague labels such as "affiliate link" alone when the
consumer may not understand that the publisher receives compensation.
The FTC specifically recommends clear and conspicuous disclosure of
affiliate relationships and emphasizes proximity to the
recommendation/link.

# 4. Personal loan affiliate guidelines

-   Do not represent a specific APR, payment, term, loan amount, or fee
    as universally available unless it actually is available on those
    terms.

-   If discussing APR or payment, preserve material assumptions such as
    loan amount, term, creditworthiness, fees, and timing where
    relevant.

-   Do not state or imply that approval is guaranteed or that an
    applicant is certain to receive a particular amount or rate.

-   Avoid claims such as "best personal loan for everyone" unless the
    basis for the statement is clearly defined and defensible.

-   When comparing lenders, use the same comparison dimensions across
    products where feasible: APR, fees, loan amount, term, funding
    timing, eligibility, and notable restrictions.

-   Do not encourage consumers to take on unnecessary debt or imply that
    borrowing is a risk-free solution to financial problems.

-   Clearly distinguish a soft credit inquiry/prequalification from a
    hard credit inquiry when describing the process, and verify the
    distinction for the specific product.

# 5. Credit card affiliate guidelines

-   Present APR, annual fee, rewards, introductory offers,
    balance-transfer terms, foreign transaction fees, and other material
    terms accurately and with applicable conditions.

-   Promotional rates or fees must not be presented in a way that hides
    the post-promotional rate or other material conditions.

-   Do not describe rewards as "free money" or imply that rewards are
    guaranteed regardless of spending, redemption, account status, or
    program rules.

-   Do not imply that a consumer will be approved for a card based only
    on a stated credit score or profile.

-   When describing welcome bonuses, state the qualifying spending
    requirement, time window, and other material conditions.

-   Avoid encouraging behavior that could reasonably cause consumers to
    incur avoidable interest, fees, or debt.

# 6. Mortgage prequalification affiliate guidelines

-   Clearly distinguish prequalification, preapproval, and a loan
    approval/commitment. Do not use these terms interchangeably.

-   Do not represent a prequalification result as a guarantee that the
    consumer will receive a mortgage, a particular rate, or a particular
    loan amount.

-   When quoting mortgage rates or payments, include the relevant
    assumptions and required context. Mortgage advertising can trigger
    specific disclosure obligations under Regulation Z.

-   Avoid claims that a consumer will definitely qualify based on
    income, credit score, home price, down payment, or another single
    factor.

-   Do not imply that prequalification guarantees acceptance of an offer
    by a seller, closing, or funding.

-   Be especially careful with statements about closing costs,
    cash-to-close, monthly payment, points, lender credits, and
    estimated property-related expenses.

# 7. Comparison and ranking guidelines

-   Define the comparison criteria before ranking products.

-   Use current product data and record the source/date for each
    material term.

-   Do not allow affiliate compensation alone to determine factual
    claims or a product\'s suitability.

-   If compensation affects placement, disclose that clearly and avoid
    presenting the placement as an objective ranking unless the
    methodology genuinely supports it.

-   Use consistent criteria across products. Do not compare one product
    using APR while comparing another using an unrelated benefit without
    explaining the methodology.

-   Separate factual product attributes from editorial judgments. For
    example, "has no annual fee" is factual; "best for most people" is
    an evaluative conclusion requiring defined criteria and
    substantiation.

-   Do not create false scarcity, urgency, or exclusivity.

# 8. Prohibited or high-risk language library

-   Guaranteed approval / guaranteed acceptance

-   Everyone qualifies

-   No matter your credit score

-   Guaranteed lowest rate / guaranteed best rate

-   Instant approval guaranteed

-   Guaranteed savings

-   Guaranteed credit-score increase

-   Risk-free borrowing

-   No downside

-   Free money

-   Guaranteed mortgage approval

-   This loan/card is right for you

-   The best product for everyone

-   Act now or you will lose your chance (unless a genuine, documented
    deadline exists)

These phrases are not necessarily unlawful in every conceivable context,
but they should be treated as high-risk for review because they can
create unsupported guarantees, omit material qualifications, or imply a
certainty that a financial product cannot provide.

# 9. Fair lending and consumer protection

-   Do not steer consumers toward or away from credit products based on
    protected characteristics or proxies for protected characteristics.

-   Recommendation logic should rely on legitimate, documented
    product-fit factors such as stated financial goals, product
    features, eligibility criteria, fees, APR, rewards, term, or other
    relevant consumer-provided preferences.

-   Avoid language suggesting that a product is intended only for, or
    inappropriate for, a protected class unless a lawful and documented
    basis exists and counsel has approved the approach.

-   Do not infer sensitive characteristics from names, images, browsing
    behavior, ZIP code, language, or other indirect signals for credit
    recommendation purposes.

-   Use consistent recommendation and evaluation criteria across
    comparable consumers.

# 10. Data, privacy, and lead-generation considerations

-   Collect only information necessary for the stated consumer
    experience and comply with applicable privacy requirements and the
    company\'s privacy notice.

-   Do not request or expose sensitive financial information
    unnecessarily in affiliate content or analytics.

-   Do not imply that an affiliate or publisher has access to
    underwriting information unless that access actually exists and is
    authorized.

-   Clearly distinguish educational content, affiliate referral flows,
    prequalification flows, and formal applications.

-   Where consumer data is transferred to a lender, issuer, broker, or
    other third party, make the recipient and purpose understandable to
    the consumer.

# 11. Source and evidence requirements

Every material product claim should ideally have a traceable source.
Recommended source hierarchy:

1.  Current official product terms, disclosures, rate tables, cardmember
    agreements, lender/issuer documentation, or approved marketing
    materials.

2.  Applicable federal and state regulatory materials.

3.  Reliable third-party sources for general educational claims, where
    appropriate.

4.  Internal product databases with documented refresh dates.

For an automated/LLM evaluation system, store claim-level evidence
whenever possible: source URL/document, retrieval date, product
identifier, relevant passage, and the product-term version used.

# 13. Automated evaluation checks

-   Affiliate disclosure present near each recommendation/link.

-   No unsupported guarantee terms such as "guaranteed," "everyone
    qualifies," or equivalent certainty.

-   Every stated APR/rate/fee/reward/term matches the current product
    source.

-   Required conditions for promotional offers are present.

-   Prequalification is not described as approval or a commitment to
    lend.

-   No invented lender, card, rate, fee, reward, eligibility rule, or
    affiliate relationship.

-   Claims have retrievable evidence and source timestamps.

-   Recommendation criteria are based on consumer/product-fit factors
    rather than compensation alone.

-   No prohibited or inappropriate protected-class targeting/steering.

-   Affiliate links resolve to the intended product/provider.

-   Disclosures and material limitations are visible on mobile and
    desktop.

-   Generated content does not imply personalized financial advice
    unless the product and compliance framework support that
    functionality.

# 14. Review workflow

**1. Product data validation:** Confirm current product terms,
eligibility, rates, fees, and promotional conditions.

**2. Content generation:** Generate or publish only from
approved/current product data and approved claim language.

**3. Automated screening:** Run deterministic checks plus LLM evaluation
against the rubric.

**4. Evidence verification:** Verify material claims against
authoritative sources.

**5. Compliance review:** Escalate high-risk financial claims,
disclosures, fairness issues, and ambiguous regulatory questions.

**6. Monitoring:** Periodically re-check affiliate pages, links, product
terms, and partner content after publication.

**7. Remediation:** Correct, remove, or pause content when material
inaccuracies or compliance issues are found.

# 15. Regulatory reference points

-   FTC Advertising and Marketing: truth-in-advertising principles
    require advertising claims to be truthful, non-deceptive/non-unfair,
    and evidence-based.

-   FTC Endorsement Guides: affiliate relationships and other material
    connections should be disclosed clearly and conspicuously;
    advertisers should train and monitor endorsers/affiliate networks.

-   CFPB Regulation Z (12 CFR Part 1026): covers consumer credit,
    including mortgages, open-end credit, and installment loans, and
    contains advertising/disclosure requirements.

-   Regulation Z §1026.24 and §1026.16: credit advertising must use
    actually available terms and comply with applicable disclosure
    requirements.

-   Regulation Z §1026.60: credit-card applications and solicitations
    have specific disclosure requirements.

-   Regulation V affiliate-marketing rules may apply when eligibility
    information received from an affiliate is used for marketing
    solicitations; consult applicable requirements before using
    affiliate-sourced eligibility information.

# 16. Implementation note for an evaluation product

For an evaluation-focused affiliate marketing application, treat the
guidelines above as a policy layer rather than only as prompt
instructions. Store structured rules alongside source evidence. A useful
internal representation is:

> claim → product → source → source_date → rule_id → severity →
> evaluator_result → human_review_status

This makes it possible to test the system on intentionally problematic
examples, measure false positives/false negatives, and compare LLM-judge
scores with deterministic compliance checks.

**Important:** This is a general working guideline, not a legal opinion.
Consumer financial advertising can be subject to federal and state
requirements, product-specific rules, lender/issuer policies, and
contractual restrictions. Have counsel/compliance review the final
affiliate program rules and any production claims before launch.
